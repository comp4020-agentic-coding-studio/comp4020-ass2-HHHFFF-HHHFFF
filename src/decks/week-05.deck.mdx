# Tenerife

## 27 March 1977 · Los Rodeos

You already hold the rule.

Today it is evidence.

---

# 583

---

## Neither aircraft should have been there

Las Palmas closed — a bomb in the terminal.

Los Rodeos took the diversions.

A small airport, full of widebodies, at 2,000 ft, in hill fog.

---

## So the runway was the taxiway

The taxiway was blocked by parked aircraft.

Departing aircraft had to **backtrack the runway** and turn off part way down.

That is why a 747 was on it.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

The phraseology was operating outside the conditions it was designed for.

---

## Four failures

1. A non-standard progress report
2. A simultaneous transmission
3. The reserved word, outside its clearance
4. A challenge with no phrase available

In that order.

---

## One — the progress report

```atc-nonstandard
KLM      We are now at takeoff.
```

*Positioned at the takeoff point?*

*Or commencing takeoff?*

Both are grammatical. Both are things an aircraft can be.

---

## And the reply

```atc-nonstandard
TWR      OK. Stand by for takeoff, I will call you.
```

`OK` is not a word this language has.

It arrived first — and it arrived as agreement to whatever the other station
believed it had said.

---

## Two — stepped on

As the tower transmitted *stand by*, Pan Am transmitted that they were still
on the runway.

The two overlapped.

In the KLM cockpit, a heterodyne where the prohibition should have been.

---

# No transmitting station could know

---

## Week 1, arriving on schedule

Simplex. One carrier, one speaker.

Press to transmit and you cannot hear.

You cannot know you were stepped on. Neither can the other station.

---

## Three — the reserved word

> Stand by for **takeoff**, I will call you.

In fog. Partly obscured. To a crew already committed.

The recommendation that followed: avoid *take-off* in departure clearances.

That is the direct ancestor of week 4.

---

## Four — the challenge

The flight engineer asked whether Pan Am was clear.

He was answered. He did not ask again.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

There was no phrase available for *I believe this clearance is not valid.*

There still isn't.

---

## Be careful with this accident

It is the most-told accident in aviation, and the telling has smoothed it.

- It is **one** accident. The rule is broader than one accident justifies.
- The captain has become a character in a story.

---

## What a language course can say

Nothing useful about his personality.

Quite a lot about a system in which the only available word for *stop*
contained the word *takeoff*.
