Today you do the examiner's job badly, which is the fastest way to understand
what it involves.

## Before you arrive

Read the ICAO rating scale descriptors for levels 3, 4 and 5 across all six
criteria. Bring them. The session is unusable without the descriptors in front
of you.

## In the session

**Calibration, fifteen minutes.** Two samples, rated as a room, out loud, with
Priya arguing the descriptors. Nobody is right yet; the point is to fix what
the words in the scale mean before using them.

**Solo rating, forty minutes.** Six samples, rated silently and individually.
All six criteria for each. Write the criterion that decided your overall
rating.

Samples are real, anonymised, and used with permission for training. Some are
non-native speakers, some are native. Do not assume the difficulty runs the
way you expect — one of the six is a native speaker whose local shorthand and
speed make comprehension harder than anything else on the tape, and the room
regularly rates that one highest.

**The spread, twenty-five minutes.** Ratings go on the board anonymously. There
will be a spread, usually two levels wide, sometimes three.

We then look at *which criterion* drove the disagreement. Almost always it is
comprehension or interactions — the two that depend on the unexpected — and
almost never pronunciation. That is the week's finding: the criteria that
matter most are the ones a booked interview measures least reliably.

**What the examiner gave them, ten minutes.** Priya reveals the operational
rating each sample actually received. It will not match your consensus on at
least two of them, and she will explain the reasoning without defending it as
obviously correct.

## What we are careful about

The samples are people's voices and people's licences. Rate the speech against
the descriptors. Do not speculate about nationality, and do not use the
samples outside the session.

If you find yourself rating an accent, stop and find the descriptor you think
it fails. Usually there isn't one, which is the point.

## Afterwards

Bring your disagreement to the audit in week 11 if it is relevant — several
past audits have proposed rating scales for their own domain, and the honest
ones cite this session as the reason they included a limits section about
assessor variance.
