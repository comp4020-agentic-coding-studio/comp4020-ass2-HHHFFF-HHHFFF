Both teachers are in this one, because the second half needs a controller and
a pilot working against each other in a way one person cannot fake.

Your transcript for the week 7 assessment is allocated at the end of this
session.

## Before you arrive

Know the three vertical systems and which pressure setting goes with each. The
session does not teach that; it drills it.

## In the session

**Number ladders, twenty minutes.** Levels, altitudes, pressure settings,
headings, squawks. Same escalating format as week 2, so you can see whether
your week 2 confusions have moved. Several people find that digits are now
fine and *units* are the problem, which is the week's point arriving on
schedule.

**Pressure exercise, twenty-five minutes.** You fly a descent through the
transition layer with a deliberately wrong QNH set and find out how far off
you are. Then the same descent with the correct setting. The gap between the
two numbers is the whole reason the phrase carries the setting.

**Sentence-type block, thirty-five minutes.** This is the new thing and it is
harder than it sounds.

Halvard transmits; you say what class of message it was before you respond to
it. Clearance, instruction, information, or request. Some are deliberately
Charkhi Dadri-shaped: traffic information containing a level, delivered at
speed, to a room that has spent twenty minutes reading back levels.

Most of the room will act on at least one advisory as though it were an
instruction. That is the finding. Having spent the previous block being drilled
to read back every number you hear is exactly the priming that makes it
happen, and the session is ordered that way on purpose.

If you want to argue that the ordering is entrapment, argue it — it is a fair
objection and the honest answer is that the real frequency does the same thing
to you and does not warn you either.

## Afterwards

**Transcript allocation.** One exchange per student, handed out at the end.
Different students get different exchanges; you may discuss method, but not
content, and the marking assumes you did not.

The classification habit from the last block is the one the assessment leans
on hardest. A deviation is easier to see once you have decided what kind of
sentence you were owed.
