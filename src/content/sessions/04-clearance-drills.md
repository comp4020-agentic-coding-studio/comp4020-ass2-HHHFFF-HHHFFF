The first assessment is due at the end of this week, and this session is the
last supervised practice before it.

## Before you arrive

Bring your confusion list from week 2 and your hearback catch rate from week
3. The drills are set up so you can work on your own pairs rather than
everybody's.

## In the session

**Clean clearances, twenty-five minutes.** Takeoff, landing, line up and wait,
conditional clearances, cancellations. Both seats. Speed increases until
somebody produces the reserved word in the wrong slot, which usually takes
about eight minutes.

**Wrong on purpose, twenty-five minutes.** You will be asked to transmit the
illegal forms — `POSITION AND HOLD`, the ambiguous progress report, a
clearance with the runway designator omitted — and the room names what each
one risks.

This part surprises people, and it is worth saying why we do it. You cannot
reliably detect a form you have never produced. A student who has only ever
heard correct phraseology recognises *unfamiliar*, not *wrong*, and
unfamiliar is exactly what a foreign accent or a busy controller also sounds
like. Producing the bad forms yourself is what separates the two.

**Assessment clinic, remaining time.** Bring your drill recording and we will
listen to it with you. Anything you fix in the room still counts; the
assessment is about the recording you submit, not about whether you needed
help making it.

## A note on the ordering

You may notice this session asks you to say things that next week's accident
killed 583 people through. That ordering is intentional and it is the same
argument the lecture makes: the forms are practised as **forms** this week, so
that next week you are not learning a rule and mourning at the same time.

If that ordering sits badly with you, say so in the session. It is a
defensible course-design decision and not an obviously correct one, and the
objection is worth hearing out loud.

## Afterwards

The readback drills are due Friday at noon. What leaves this session is either
a submission you are happy with, or a conversation about an extension — the
second is fine and the policies page says how.
