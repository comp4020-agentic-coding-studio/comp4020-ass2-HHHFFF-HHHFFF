Today you read the reports rather than a summary of them. Bring a laptop.

## Before you arrive

Nothing. The reading happens in the session on purpose — the point is to watch
yourself read a primary source, not to arrive having already been told what it
says.

## In the session

**Forty minutes reading, silently.** Half the room takes the SKYbrary findings
and recommendations; the other half takes the ALPA human factors report. Read
the findings, not the narrative.

**Thirty minutes, the mapping table.** Three columns, filled in as a room. One
row is done for you as the worked example:

| Finding | Phrase that exists now | Fully covered? |
|---|---|---|
| "Take-off" used in a departure clearance | `DEPARTURE` everywhere but the clearance itself | Yes — directly |
| Simultaneous transmission obscured a prohibition | — | No phrase; mitigated by equipment |
| Crew member doubted the clearance and did not press | — | Nothing at all |

For each finding, does today's phraseology contain something that addresses
it? Sometimes yes and precisely — the reserved-word rule. Sometimes yes but
only partly. And sometimes the answer is **nothing**, and that last column is
the one worth the session.

**Twenty minutes on the empty rows.** A finding with no corresponding phrase
is one of three things: something a phrase cannot fix, something a phrase
could fix that nobody has built, or something the industry decided to fix
another way — training, procedure, equipment. Deciding which is a judgement
and you will disagree with each other.

The flight engineer's challenge is the row that always ends up empty. There is
still no standard phrase for *I think the clearance we are acting on is not
valid*. Whether that is a gap or a category error is an open argument, and
week 12 returns to it.

## Where the two reports disagree

They do, and finding it is part of the exercise. The Spanish investigation and
the ALPA report weight the contributing factors differently, and ALPA has an
obvious interest — it is a pilots' association writing about a pilot.

That is not a reason to discount it. It is a reason to read both and notice
that "the primary source" is not one document with one view, which is a habit
you need for the transcript analysis in two weeks.

## Afterwards

Your mapping table is your own notes and is not collected. The habit it builds
is the one the week 7 assessment marks: name the deviation against a specific
standard, or admit there isn't one.

voices. If you would rather not be in the room for the audio in the week 5
