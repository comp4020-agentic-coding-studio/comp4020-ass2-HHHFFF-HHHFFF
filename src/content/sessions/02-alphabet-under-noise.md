The alphabet is easy in a quiet room, which is why we do not practise it in
one.

## Before you arrive

Learn the twenty-six words and the ten digits. This is the only rote learning
the course asks of you, and the session assumes it is done — we are not
spending the hour teaching you that S is `SIERRA`.

## In the session

**Ladder drill, clean channel.** Callsigns at increasing speed until you
fumble. We record where each person breaks. Nobody breaks in the same place,
which is the point of doing it individually rather than as a class chant.

**Ladder drill, degraded channel.** The same, with pink noise mixed into the
headset at three increasing levels. Your personal confusion pairs will show up
here, and they are usually not the ones you predicted. Write down what you
actually mis-heard, not what you expected to.

**Number blocks.** Levels, headings, squawks, frequencies. Digit-by-digit, and
then deliberately wrongly as a group — *twenty-seven left* — so you can hear
how much faster the correct form is to parse even though it has more syllables
in it.

**Copy under load.** Full clearances, noise up, while doing something else
with your hands. This is the closest the session gets to the real thing.

## The counter-example we do on purpose

Late in the session Halvard will transmit this. It is wrong in two ways, and
you are asked to name both before we discuss it:

```atc-nonstandard
TWR      Cessna GBAME, taxi to holding point A2, okay?
GBAME    Okay, roger.
```

The letters are unspelled and `OKAY` is not a word this language has. What
makes it worth doing as a drill rather than a slide is that it sounds
completely fine. It is friendly, it is quick, and everybody in the room will
have understood it — which is precisely why week 7 argues that the harm of
non-standard phraseology is invisible from inside a successful exchange.

## Afterwards

Your personal confusion list is the thing that leaves this session, and you
keep it for the rest of the semester. It also feeds the first assessment: the
readback drills in week 4 are marked partly on whether you have addressed the
pairs you identified here.
