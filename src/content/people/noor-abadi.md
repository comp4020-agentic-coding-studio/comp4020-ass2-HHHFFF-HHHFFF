Trained as a phonetician, then spent eleven years as a contract analyst on
cockpit voice recordings: the person an investigation calls when the finding
turns on whether a syllable was there.

That work is where this course came from. An investigator asks *what was
said*. The interesting question, almost always, is *what the words were
designed to prevent, and how the design failed anyway* — and nobody was asking
it, because phraseology sits in the gap between linguistics, which finds it
boring, and aviation, which finds it settled.

## What to ask about

The design arguments: why a phrase is worded the way it is, who decided, what
the alternative was, and what evidence was available when they chose. Bring a
transmission you think is badly designed. That is the whole course, and a good
counter-example is worth more to it than a good essay.
