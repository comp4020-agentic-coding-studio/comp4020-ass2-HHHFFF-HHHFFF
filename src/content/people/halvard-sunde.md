Twenty-two years as an area controller, almost all of it on the same en-route
sector. Retired from the operational roster, not from the frequency: still
holds a rating, and works the controller position in most of this course's sim
sessions.

Brings the half of the loop that the pilot-facing literature tends to leave
out. Readback is the part everyone teaches. **Hearback** — the controller's
duty to notice that the readback was wrong, under the traffic load that made
the error likely in the first place — is the part that fails, and it is
difficult to teach from a transcript because the transcript has no workload in
it.

## What to ask about

What a frequency actually sounds like when it is busy. Why controllers depart
from standard phraseology, and which departures are worth arguing about. What
it is like to hear a wrong readback and have to decide, in about a second,
whether it matters.

## A standing disagreement

Week 7 argues that non-standard phraseology is a defect. That position is not
unanimous among the people teaching this course, and the lecture will say so
rather than smoothing it over. Twenty-two years of doing the job is a data
source, and so is the accident record; where they disagree, the disagreement
is the interesting part.
