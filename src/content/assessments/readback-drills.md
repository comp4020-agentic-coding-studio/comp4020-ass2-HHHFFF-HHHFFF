> Read back twelve clearances correctly, and show that you know where your own
> ear is unreliable.

The first assessment is deliberately small and deliberately early. Fifteen per
cent, in week 4, on a mechanical skill — because the rest of the course argues
about design, and it is worth establishing first that you can actually operate
the thing being argued about.

## What you submit

**One audio file.** You will be given a sheet of twelve clearances in week 4's
sim session. Record yourself reading back all twelve, in the order given, once
each. No editing between clearances; a single continuous take per clearance is
fine, and restarting a clearance is fine, but submit the attempt you want
marked rather than a splice of best syllables.

**One short note, under 200 words.** Name the confusion pairs you identified
in week 2, and say what you did about them. "Nothing, they did not recur" is
an acceptable answer if it is true.

Both go in the submission folder by Friday noon.

## How it is marked

**Accuracy of readback (50).** Does the readback contain every element of the
clearance, in the right order, with no element that was not in the clearance?
A digit wrong is the whole criterion for that clearance.

**Standard phraseology (30).** Reserved words in their reserved places.
`AFFIRM` not *affirmative*, `SAY AGAIN` not *sorry?*, and no `TAKE-OFF`
outside a takeoff clearance. The glossary is the reference and it is the
authority for this criterion.

**Response to your own confusion list (20).** This is the criterion that is
not about the recording. Twenty per cent is here rather than in accuracy
because the course's position is that knowing your own failure modes is worth
more than a clean take. A student with two remaining confusions who has
identified and worked on them scores better here than one who submitted
something flawless and wrote "no confusions".

## What this is not marked on

Accent, fluency, speed, or how much you sound like a pilot. None of those are
criteria and none of them will be commented on. If you want the reasoning, it
is in week 8: a rating scale for human language has consequences, and this
course is not qualified to apply one to a first-year student.

## If you cannot record

Say so before Friday and the drill can be done live in a booked slot instead,
at no penalty. The audio file is a convenience for marking, not the thing
being assessed.
