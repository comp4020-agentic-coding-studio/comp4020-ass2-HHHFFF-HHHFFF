> Ten minutes on frequency. Both seats. At least one clearance you have never
> heard.

## Why this is holistic

The other three assessments are weighted, with criteria you can see and aim
at. This one is not, and the reason is worth stating rather than hiding.

A checklist for live radio work would be gameable in the wrong direction. A
student optimising for a rubric on frequency will produce clean-sounding
transmissions and will guess rather than admit a gap, because guessing sounds
better against every criterion you could write down. That is precisely the
behaviour the whole semester argues against.

So it is judged as one thing, against one question: **hand this person an
unfamiliar clearance on a busy frequency — do they either understand it or
visibly not understand it?** The failure mode is not being wrong. It is being
wrong invisibly.

## What happens in the ten minutes

**Both seats.** Five minutes flying, five minutes working the controller
position. Week 3 established that hearback is the half that fails, so a check
that only tested you as a pilot would test half the course.

**One unfamiliar clearance,** at least. Something not drilled: a conditional,
a reroute, an unusual constraint, a phrase from a region you have not worked.
This is where the assessment actually happens.

**Whatever else the frequency is doing.** Other students are on it.

## What counts in your favour

- `SAY AGAIN`, used when you need it.
- Reading back the part you got and naming the part you did not.
- `UNABLE`, when you are.
- Stopping a readback partway to correct it with `CORRECTION`.

## What counts against

- A fluent readback of something you did not understand.
- Silence where a query belonged.
- Reserved words out of place.
- Filling a gap with what you expected the clearance to be — the exact failure
  you measured in yourself in week 3.

## Booking and rebooking

The rule is at the top of this page because missing it costs you the
component. The rest of it is here.

Slots open in [week 10](/sessions/10-datalink-only/) and are booked in that
session. They run through the week after the
[week 12 presentations](/sessions/12-presentations/), so there is more room
than there are students and no advantage in booking first.

Rebooking is free, needs no reason, and has no limit, as long as you do it
before the slot you hold. That is deliberate: a rebooking policy that costs
something teaches students to turn up to a check they are not ready for, and
a check you sit unprepared measures nothing anybody wanted to know.

Not appearing without rebooking is a non-submission. It cannot be late the
way a file can, so the fix is a new slot rather than a penalty — see
[policies](/policies/), and tell us sooner rather than later.

## After the check

You get a paragraph, not a score breakdown, within five working days —
consistent with it being one judgement rather than a sum of parts. If you want
the reasoning behind that paragraph, ask, and you will get it in full.
