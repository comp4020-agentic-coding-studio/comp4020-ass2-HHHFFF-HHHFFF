> Here is four minutes of real audio. Say what was said, say what was wrong
> with it, and write it up so somebody who thinks you are wrong can check.

## What you submit

**A transcript** of the assigned exchange — one per student, allocated in week
6 — with timings, station labels and the notation used across this site.
Include the parts you could not make out, marked as unintelligible. A gap
honestly marked is worth more than a confident guess, and the marking reflects
that.

**A diagnosis.** For each deviation from standard phraseology: what was said,
what the standard form is, and what the deviation risked in that specific
exchange. Not in general — in that one.

**A finding**, 600–800 words. One claim about the exchange, argued.

## How it is marked

**Accuracy of transcription (25).** Against the marker's own transcript.
Honestly marked gaps do not lose marks; invented content does.

**Diagnosis against the standard (40).** Each claimed deviation has to be
named against a specific phrase in the glossary or in ICAO phraseology. "This
was non-standard" is not a diagnosis. "This used `ROGER` where `AFFIRM` was
required, so an acknowledgement of receipt was recorded as an agreement" is.

The heaviest criterion, because it is the course's central skill and the one
that most often collapses into taste.

**Quality of the written finding (35).** The test is checkability. Can a
reader who disagrees with you find the thing you relied on and evaluate it
themselves? That means quoting the transmission, citing the standard, and
being explicit about the step where you moved from what was said to what it
risked.

A finding that is right for reasons the reader cannot follow scores below a
finding that is arguable and fully exposed.

## The trap in this assessment

Most first attempts diagnose the transmission that sounds most unusual. That
is usually an accent, a regionalism or a busy controller clipping words — none
of which is a deviation from the standard.

Meanwhile the actual deviation is often perfectly fluent and sounds fine,
which is week 7's whole argument. Fluency is not compliance. Check the forms,
not the delivery.

## Use of the audio

The exchanges are drawn from published accident and incident dockets and from
recordings released for training. They involve real people, some of whom died.
Write about the transmission and the system that shaped it. Do not write about
the character of the person transmitting, and do not name individuals where
the source does not.
